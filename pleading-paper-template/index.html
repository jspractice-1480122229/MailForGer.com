Why this exists

Most "pleading paper" templates in circulation are Word-format files from county law libraries, a circa-2005 OpenOffice template, paid template services, or LaTeX attempts that don't correctly conform to the rules. None of those were a current, actively maintained, rules-current open-source ODT template — so this was built from the primary source rule text: California Rules of Court 2.100–2.119 and C.D. Cal. Local Rule 11.

Both templates generate the line-number column and the body-text grid from a single shared table — one row per ruled line — instead of two independently spaced elements tuned to visually match. That avoids a real, observed failure mode: line numbers and body text drifting out of alignment after the first page.

How to use

  1. Download the .ott file for your court.
  2. Open it in LibreOffice Writer — it loads as a new document based on the template.
  3. Fill in the attorney/party block, case caption, and body text. Line numbers and page layout are already handled.
  4. For pleadings longer than two pages, copy the continuation-page table onto additional pages.
